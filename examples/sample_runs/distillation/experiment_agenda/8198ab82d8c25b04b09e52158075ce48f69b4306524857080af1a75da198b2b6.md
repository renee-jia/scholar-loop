Status: **EXECUTED**

Protocol hash: `8198ab82d8c25b04b09e52158075ce48f69b4306524857080af1a75da198b2b6`

Purpose: falsification

An off-to-on switch fired by the student's own free-run invalid rate lowers val_invalid_rate. The trigger's response to the student's state matters, not only the step where it fires: a yoked replay at the same step on a fresh seed should not match it. The benefit needs the student's own prefixes: ground-truth prefixes corrupted at the student's measured per-token error rate should not reproduce it.

Expected: Over seeds 0-4, adaptive_tau25 (probe invalid < 25%, 1500-step cap, then on_policy_frac=1.0) has a mean val_invalid_rate at least 1.0 point below both off_policy_ref and pure_on_policy, and below the 8.8 hard-label baseline. It is also no worse than fixed_switch_1500. yoked_replay is within noise of adaptive_tau25 or worse than it. corrupt_prefix recovers less than half of the adaptive gain over off_policy_ref. Per-seed diagnostics show the switch step and a nonzero number of on-policy batches only after that step.

Falsifying outcome: The claim is falsified if either of two things happens. (1) adaptive_tau25 does not beat the better of off_policy_ref and pure_on_policy by more than the seed standard deviation. (2) yoked_replay matches adaptive_tau25 and corrupt_prefix matches it too, meaning it recovers half or more of the gain. Outcome (2) would mean that step timing plus generic prefix noise explain the effect. A trigger that fires only through the cap on every seed means the adaptive mechanism was never exercised. In that case adaptive_tau25 is equivalent to a fixed 1500 switch, and the responsiveness claim is untested rather than supported.

## Limitations

- Only one adaptive setting (tau=25%, target frac=1.0) and one fixed step (1500) are run. The tau and target-frac sweep and the 750/2250 fixed steps are omitted, so 'best tau' is not identified.
- The yoked arm must train on a different seed (seed+1000). With the same seed, its training stream would be bit-identical to adaptive's, because the probe consumes no training RNG. Its pairing with the reference is therefore weaker, and seed variance enters the yoked-versus-adaptive contrast.
- The yoked arm spends extra compute replaying the adaptive run up to the firing step, at most 1500 steps. The corrupt arm samples student rollouts it then discards. Forward-pass counts are reported only as on_policy_batches, probe_calls and replay_steps, not as a single compute-matched total.
- The reference uses the unmodified script and runs no probe, so it has no probe trajectory. The probe consumes no training RNG and does not change training in the edited arms.
- Per-token error is measured against the nearest valid mode on sampled (temperature 1) student rollouts. This is a lower bound on true deviation and only approximates the student's error structure. Uniform substitution does not reproduce the positional or mode-mixing pattern of real student errors.
- The probe uses training prompts, not val, so the trigger may track val_invalid_rate imperfectly.
- The trigger is checked only every 100 steps, so the switch step is quantised to multiples of 100.
- Self-reported diagnostics come from train.py and are not verified by the scorer.

## What each arm isolates

- **off_policy_ref** (reference): Unmodified default (pure off-policy). The baseline for all paired differences.
- **pure_on_policy** (control): Against the reference: the effect of student-generated prefixes for the whole run, with no schedule.
- **fixed_switch_1500** (control): Against adaptive_tau25: whether a state-triggered switch beats a fixed mid-run switch step.
- **adaptive_tau25** (treatment): Against the reference and pure_on_policy: the value of an invalid-rate-triggered off-to-on switch.
- **yoked_replay** (control): Against adaptive_tau25: online responsiveness to the student's own state versus merely the switch step location.
- **corrupt_prefix** (control): Against adaptive_tau25: student-specific error correction versus generic prefix noise at a matched per-token error rate.
