# Does an Adaptive Off-to-On-Policy Switch Help Distillation? A Pre-Registered Negative Result on a Multimodal Transduction Task

> **Panel review:** reject · mean score 2.2/10 (harsh-but-fair 2 · harsh-critical 2 · open-minded 2.5)

## Abstract

On the multimodal-transduction-L6-A8 seq-distill task, we asked whether switching from off-policy to on-policy training, once the student's own probe invalid rate falls below a threshold, lowers val_invalid_rate compared with pure off-policy, pure on-policy and a fixed-step switch [scope_question]. The pre-registered falsifying outcome was met: the adaptive-switch arm did not beat the better pure regime, so the experiment does not distinguish the switching rules [claim_hypothesis_not_supported]. We ran one pre-registered protocol with six arms, each over five seed–fold pairs, with the same learning rate, temperature, forward-KL loss and step budget in every arm and val_invalid_rate (lower is better) computed by a frozen scorer [scope_method]. According to self-reported diagnostics, the adaptive trigger fired before the forced-switch cap on all five seed–fold pairs, so the adaptive mechanism was exercised in this run [claim_trigger_exercised]. The adaptive arm still did not beat pure_on_policy on mean val_invalid_rate, which averaged 12.88 for the adaptive arm against 9.04 for pure_on_policy [claim_adaptive_not_below_pure_on].

## Introduction

The task, multimodal-transduction-L6-A8, gives the student an input of L=6 symbols from an alphabet of 8, and three outputs are valid: the copy, the reversal, and the reversal incremented mod 8 [setup_task]. An output that mixes modes counts as invalid [setup_task].

GKD trains students on a mixture of fixed and self-generated sequences with a static student-data fraction [related_gkd]. We keep that fraction knob and switch it once during training, conditioned on the student's probe invalid rate [related_gkd]. DASH-OPD and DTDD switch adaptively between teacher and student within rollouts using discrepancy signals, and their abstracts do not describe yoked or corrupted-prefix controls [related_adaptive_triggers]. Our study instead makes a single, one-way, training-schedule switch gated by a probe invalid rate [related_adaptive_triggers]. Instance-level curriculum distillation orders training instances using an earlier student snapshot, whereas we schedule the policy that generates prefixes rather than the order of instances [related_curriculum].

We ask whether, on the multimodal-transduction-L6-A8 seq-distill task, switching from off-policy to on-policy training when the student's own probe invalid rate falls below a threshold lowers val_invalid_rate compared with pure off-policy, pure on-policy and a fixed-step switch [scope_question]. If it does, we ask whether the benefit depends on the trigger responding to the student's state, and on the student's own prefixes [scope_question].

The registered prediction was that adaptive_tau25 is at least 1.0 point below both pure regimes and below the 8.8 hard-label baseline, and no worse than fixed_switch_1500 [setup_prediction]. The prediction further held that yoked_replay is within noise of adaptive or worse, and that corrupt_prefix recovers less than half of the adaptive gain over off_policy_ref [setup_prediction]. The registered falsification states that the hypothesis fails if adaptive_tau25 does not beat the better of off_policy_ref and pure_on_policy by more than the seed standard deviation, or if both yoked_replay and corrupt_prefix match it [setup_falsification]. A trigger that fires only through the cap on every seed would leave responsiveness untested [setup_falsification].

The pre-registered falsifying outcome was met: pure_on_policy had the lower mean (9.04 against 12.88), so the adaptive switch did not beat the better pure regime by more than the seed–fold spread [claim_hypothesis_not_supported]. The yoked-replay and corrupted-prefix controls did not implement their intended contrasts, so the conservative reading is that this experiment does not distinguish the switching rules [claim_hypothesis_not_supported]. The prediction that the adaptive switch would sit below both pure regimes by a margin therefore fails for the on-policy endpoint of val_invalid_rate [claim_adaptive_not_below_pure_on]. The adaptive arm did have the narrower spread across seed–fold pairs [claim_adaptive_not_below_pure_on].

## Related Work

Sequence-level knowledge distillation [arXiv:1606.07947] trains the student on teacher-generated outputs and is the canonical off-policy baseline [related_seqkd]. Our reference arm follows this off-policy setting and applies word-level distillation on teacher-sampled prefixes [related_seqkd].

Imitation-learning distillation [arXiv:2009.07253] [doi:10.1007/s00521-025-11162-0] treats the teacher as an oracle on student-visited states, which addresses exposure bias [related_imitation]. We designed a corrupted-prefix control to test this exposure-bias account [related_imitation]. The control's corruption rate tracked its own student's rollout error instead of the adaptive student's, so the control yields no conclusion and the account remains untested here [related_imitation].

GKD [arXiv:2306.13649] trains students on a mixture of fixed and self-generated sequences, with a static student-data fraction [related_gkd]. We keep that fraction knob and switch it once during training, conditioned on the student's probe invalid rate [related_gkd]. DASH-OPD [arXiv:2607.29078] and DTDD [arXiv:2610.19302] instead switch adaptively between teacher and student within rollouts, using discrepancy signals [related_adaptive_triggers]. Our switch is a single, one-way change in the training schedule, gated by a probe invalid rate [related_adaptive_triggers]. The abstracts of these two methods do not describe yoked or corrupted-prefix controls [related_adaptive_triggers].

Instance-level curriculum distillation [arXiv:2106.10885v1] orders training instances using an earlier student snapshot [related_curriculum]. Our study schedules the policy that generates prefixes and leaves the order of instances unchanged [related_curriculum].

MiniLLM [arXiv:2306.08543] changes two factors at once: the divergence, which becomes reverse KL, and the sampling distribution [related_minillm]. We hold the forward-KL loss fixed and vary only the prefix source and its schedule [related_minillm]. A controlled study on GSM8K [arXiv:2605.22731v1] argues that the state distribution under supervision matters alongside the loss [related_state_distribution]. Our study varies only the state source, and does so on a non-language transduction task [related_state_distribution].

Prior work [arXiv:2409.12512] reports that student-generated outputs are costly and hard to optimise when the student is much smaller than the teacher [related_capacity]. We use one fixed small student and do not vary the capacity gap [related_capacity]. Entropy-aware on-policy distillation [arXiv:2603.07079] reports that reverse-KL signals become unstable under high teacher entropy [related_entropy]. Our teacher models a three-mode distribution, but we did not measure or vary teacher entropy [related_entropy].

Mask Forcing [arXiv:2609.09123] perturbs student self-rollouts in video diffusion distillation [related_mask_forcing]. Our corrupted-prefix arm perturbs ground-truth discrete prefixes and serves only as a control, with no claim as a method [related_mask_forcing].

## Setup and Pre-Registered Protocol

We ran one pre-registered falsification protocol with six arms, each at full fidelity over five seed–fold pairs [scope_method]. Seed i was trained on the training prompts of cross-validation fold i and evaluated on that fold's val prompts, so seed and fold are confounded and no arm was evaluated on a single fold [scope_method]. Learning rate, temperature, forward-KL loss and step budget were the same in every arm, and the student architecture and teacher were frozen [scope_method]. The arms were off_policy_ref (the reference), pure_on_policy, a fixed switch to fully on-policy at a mid-run step, and an adaptive arm that switches to fully on-policy once the greedy invalid rate on a training-prompt probe batch drops below threshold, with a forced-switch cap [scope_method]. Two further arms completed the set: a yoked replay that switches at the adaptive arm's firing step but trains on a different seed, and a corrupted-prefix arm that uses the same trigger but replaces post-switch prefixes with ground truth under token substitution [scope_method]. The trusted metric is val_invalid_rate from the frozen scorer, and the analysis reports descriptive paired mean differences against off_policy_ref, with no inferential test [scope_method]. Probe trajectories, switch steps and on-policy batch counts are self-reported by train.py and are not verified by the scorer [scope_method]. The author decided to run no further experiments, so this revision narrows the argument: two control comparisons and one mechanism hypothesis are withdrawn as claims and moved to the limitations [scope_method].

In the task multimodal-transduction-L6-A8, each input is L=6 symbols from an alphabet of 8 [setup_task]. Three outputs are valid: the copy, the reversal, and the reversal incremented mod 8 [setup_task]. A mixture of modes counts as invalid [setup_task]. Every possible input is shuffled once, and five folds of 500 prompts each are cut from the start of that order [setup_folds]. The next 2000 prompts form the held-out test set, and the teacher trains on the rest [setup_folds]. A run on one fold validates on that fold and trains on the prompts of the other four [setup_folds]. The metric val_invalid_rate is the percentage of greedy student decodes on a validation fold that match none of the valid outputs, and lower is better [setup_metric]. Each validation fold holds 500 prompts [setup_metric].

The student is a frozen-architecture pre-norm causal transformer with d_model 32, 2 layers and 2 heads [setup_models]. The frozen teacher has d_model 96 and 2 layers, trained for 8000 steps on inputs disjoint from the fold pool and the test set [setup_models]. The teacher is trained once with a fixed seed for 8000 steps of AdamW at lr 1e-3 and batch 128 [setup_teacher_training]. Each teacher training target is one valid output chosen uniformly at random from the three modes, and the loss is cross-entropy over output positions [setup_teacher_training].

Every arm trains for 3000 steps with batch 64 using AdamW at lr 0.003 [setup_training]. The loss is forward KL between teacher and student next-token distributions at temperature 1.0 over output positions [setup_training]. Training prefixes are sampled rather than decoded greedily: each batch draws its output sequences from either the teacher or the student at sampling temperature 1.0 [setup_prefix_sampling]. The quantity on_policy_frac is the probability that a batch uses the student [setup_prefix_sampling].

The probe batch is a fixed subset of 256 training prompts, drawn once per run from a generator seeded with 10000 plus the run seed [setup_probe]. The probe and the evaluation decode greedily [setup_probe]. The off_policy_ref arm runs the unmodified script with on_policy_frac 0, so the teacher samples every training prefix [setup_arm_offpolicy]. The pure_on_policy arm sets on_policy_frac to 1 throughout, so the student samples every prefix and the teacher labels it [setup_arm_pureon]. The fixed_switch_1500 arm uses on_policy_frac 0 before step 1500 and 1.0 afterwards, with a probe every 100 steps for logging only [setup_arm_fixed].

In the adaptive_tau25 arm, a probe greedily decodes 256 training prompts every 100 steps [setup_arm_adaptive]. When the probe invalid rate falls below 25%, on_policy_frac switches from 0 to 1.0 [setup_arm_adaptive]. The switch is forced at step 1500 if the trigger has not fired [setup_arm_adaptive]. The yoked_replay arm first replays the adaptive run for the seed to find its firing step [setup_arm_yoked]. It then trains a fresh student from training seed seed+1000 and switches to on_policy_frac 1.0 at exactly that step, ignoring the probe [setup_arm_yoked].

The corrupt_prefix arm uses the adaptive trigger and cap [setup_arm_corrupt]. After the switch, it samples student rollouts to measure per-token error p, then trains on ground-truth outputs (mode chosen uniformly) with each symbol substituted at rate p [setup_arm_corrupt]. Per-token error p is the smallest number of positions at which a sampled student rollout differs from any valid output, divided by the output length [setup_token_error]. It is computed on each post-switch batch, and corruption draws use a generator seeded with 20000 plus the run seed [setup_token_error].

Each arm ran five seeds (zero through four) over five cross-validation folds at full fidelity [setup_seeds_folds]. Run records list fold indices alongside seed indices, so seed and fold vary together [setup_seeds_folds]. Each arm's run record lists 5 training attempts for its five seeds [setup_attempts]. Each full-fidelity run had a time budget of 1200 seconds [setup_budget]. The frozen protocol records the software environment: Python 3.12.9, torch 2.11.0 and numpy 2.2.6 [setup_environment].

The analysis computes, for each arm, per-seed paired mean differences against off_policy_ref, read descriptively beside the seed spread, with no inferential test [setup_analysis]. The profile declares a val_invalid_rate of 8.8 for a hard-label training baseline [setup_baseline]. That baseline was not re-run in this protocol [setup_baseline].

The registered prediction was that adaptive_tau25 is at least 1.0 point below both pure regimes and below the 8.8 hard-label baseline, and no worse than fixed_switch_1500 [setup_prediction]. It further predicted that yoked_replay is within noise of adaptive or worse, and that corrupt_prefix recovers less than half of the adaptive gain over off_policy_ref [setup_prediction]. Under the registered falsification, the hypothesis fails if adaptive_tau25 does not beat the better of off_policy_ref and pure_on_policy by more than the seed standard deviation, or if both yoked_replay and corrupt_prefix match it [setup_falsification]. A trigger that fires only through the cap on every seed leaves responsiveness untested [setup_falsification].

Attempts made outside the registered protocol, if any, are not reported [limit_15]. Hardware is not recorded, so the time budget is not tied to a machine [limit_15]. Setup details absent from the setup statements are not part of the recorded evidence [limit_15]. Implementation details not restated in the setup statements are fixed in the frozen scorer and the versioned training scripts, and are not described further here [limit_16].

## Results

Mean validation invalid rate (%, lower is better) over five seeds, with seed SD in parentheses, was 14.0 (3.234192) for off_policy_ref, 9.04 (8.478679) for pure_on_policy, 15.0 (3.896152) for fixed_switch_1500, 12.88 (2.229798) for adaptive_tau25, 11.0 (8.866792) for yoked_replay and 12.76 (6.582401) for corrupt_prefix [claim_all_arms_observation].

The precondition for testing trigger responsiveness held: according to self-reported diagnostics, the adaptive trigger fired before the forced-switch cap on all five seed–fold pairs, and no switch was forced [claim_trigger_exercised]. On-policy batches occurred only after the reported switch step [claim_trigger_exercised]. The adaptive mechanism was therefore exercised in this run, rather than every switch coming from the cap [claim_trigger_exercised].

The pre-registered prediction that the adaptive switch would sit below both pure regimes by a margin fails for the on-policy endpoint, because pure_on_policy averaged 9.04 and the adaptive arm averaged 12.88 [claim_adaptive_not_below_pure_on]. The adaptive arm had the narrower spread across seed–fold pairs [claim_adaptive_not_below_pure_on].

Against off_policy_ref, the same prediction meets only a small gain: the adaptive arm had a lower mean val_invalid_rate (12.88 against 14.0), a paired mean difference of -1.12 [claim_adaptive_vs_offpolicy]. The per-seed paired differences were small and mixed in sign, with four of the five seed–fold pairs within about one point of zero and one of those four positive [claim_adaptive_vs_offpolicy]. Most of the mean improvement comes from the single pair where off_policy_ref had its highest value (18.4) [claim_adaptive_vs_offpolicy]. The improvement is therefore small relative to the seed–fold spread and is not robust across pairs [claim_adaptive_vs_offpolicy].

The pure on-policy endpoint had a paired mean difference of -4.96 against off_policy_ref, but the per-pair pattern was heterogeneous [claim_pure_on_high_variance]. On three of five seed–fold pairs it was much better than off-policy, reaching values of 4.6, 2.6 and 4.2 [claim_pure_on_high_variance]. On one pair it was about equal, and on the remaining pair it was clearly worse (23.2 against the reference's 15.8) [claim_pure_on_high_variance]. This per-pair count corrects an earlier wording that placed the mean advantage on a 'minority' of seeds, since three of five pairs were much better, one about equal and one worse [limit_5]. Because the direction is not uniform and the spread is large, the sign of the mean difference is not established at this sample size [claim_pure_on_high_variance].

The prediction that the adaptive arm would fall below the profile's declared hard-label baseline is not met, since no arm's mean reached that baseline [limit_12]. That baseline was not re-measured in this study, so the comparison is indicative only [limit_12].

For the prediction that the adaptive arm would be no worse than the fixed switch, the reference point is weak: the fixed-step switch arm had the highest mean val_invalid_rate of all arms (15.0), a paired mean difference of 1.0 against off_policy_ref [claim_fixed_switch_worse]. Its paired differences were mixed in sign, with three of the five seed–fold pairs worse than the reference and two better, and the mean difference lies within the seed–fold spread [claim_fixed_switch_worse]. The direction of the fixed-step arm relative to pure off-policy is therefore not established [claim_fixed_switch_worse]. The adaptive arm (12.88) had a lower mean than the fixed-step arm, which formally satisfies the prediction that adaptive is no worse than the fixed switch [claim_fixed_switch_worse]. This contrast confounds trigger responsiveness with switch timing, because self-reported diagnostics show the adaptive switches fired earlier than the fixed step on every seed, so the adaptive arm spent more of training on-policy [claim_fixed_switch_worse].

## Discussion

The pre-registered falsifying outcome was met [claim_hypothesis_not_supported]. The adaptive-switch arm did not beat the better pure regime: pure_on_policy had the lower mean (9.04 against 12.88), so the adaptive switch was not better than the better pure regime by more than the seed–fold spread [claim_hypothesis_not_supported]. The prediction that the adaptive arm would beat both pure regimes is therefore not supported in this configuration [claim_hypothesis_not_supported]. The two controls meant to test why a trigger might help (yoked replay and corrupted prefixes) did not implement their intended contrasts and support no conclusion either way, as the limitations explain [claim_hypothesis_not_supported]. The conservative reading is that this experiment does not distinguish the switching rules [claim_hypothesis_not_supported].

The adaptive versus fixed-switch contrast is confounded with total on-policy duration [limit_8]. A competing explanation remains untested: the pure-on-policy mean advantage could come from some seeds committing to a single consistent output mode, which greedy decoding rewards, rather than from correcting exposure bias [limit_14].

## Limitations

Seed i was trained and evaluated on cross-validation fold i, so training prompts, probe prompts and the val set all change with the seed [limit_1]. The spread across seeds is therefore seed-plus-fold variation, and nothing was evaluated on a single fold [limit_1]. Paired differences are paired against the reference on both seed and fold [limit_1].

Each arm has only five seed–fold pairs, and we ran no inferential test and computed no interval on any paired difference [limit_6]. Each value comes from a single evaluation on a few hundred val prompts, and the binomial evaluation noise is of the same order as the adaptive-versus-reference mean difference [limit_6]. 'Within noise' was never quantified [limit_6].

We ran only one adaptive setting (one probe threshold, full on-policy target) and one fixed switch step [limit_7]. The threshold sweep, partial targets, the static mixed-fraction arm and the other fixed steps were omitted [limit_7]. The yoked and corrupt arms also do not match the other arms in compute: the yoked arm spends replay compute and the corrupt arm discards student rollouts [limit_9]. No compute-matched total was recorded [limit_9].

Probe trajectories, switch steps, on-policy batch counts and per-token error rates are self-reported by train.py and are not verified by the scorer [limit_10]. The probe uses training prompts, not val [limit_10]. The study covers a single synthetic task, a single tiny fixed student and a single divergence, and the results do not generalise beyond this setting [limit_11].

Run verdicts are recorded as 'discarded' and the protocol outcome as 'unreviewed' [limit_13]. The author has decided not to run further experiments, so the requested reruns (cross-yoked seeds, replayed-rate corruption, mode-share diagnostics, more seeds) were not done [limit_13].

We withdraw the claim that the corrupted-prefix control recovers the adaptive gain (claim_corrupt_recovers_gain), because that control set its substitution rate from its own student's sampled rollout error instead of the adaptive student's [limit_2]. That student drifted to higher rollout error than the adaptive student on every seed (self-reported student_rollout_tokerr_mean), so the control did not implement the specified matched noise level [limit_2]. Its post-switch prefixes were also exact ground truth rather than teacher samples, and its mean paired gain is dominated by the same outlying reference pair as the adaptive gain [limit_2]. The control is no longer treated as a test of whether the benefit needs the student's own prefixes, and that question is unanswered [limit_2].

We withdraw the claim that the yoked replay arm matches or beats the adaptive arm (claim_yoked_matches_or_beats), because the yoked arm trained on different seeds from the reference and adaptive arms [limit_3]. Its recorded per-seed differences are therefore not paired observations, and only a difference of means can be reported [limit_3]. That difference does not separate yoked from adaptive given the yoked arm's large spread, and the yoked students switched in different training states from their adaptive sources [limit_3]. Whether online responsiveness adds value beyond switch timing is untested [limit_3].

We also withdraw the variance-mechanism hypothesis (claim_variance_mechanism_hypothesis), which in the previous version proposed that more training on student-generated or noisy prefixes yields more seed-dependent outcomes [limit_4]. The data contradict this premise: the adaptive and yoked arms had identical on-policy batch counts yet very different spreads [limit_4]. With five values per arm, the spread estimates cannot rank variance [limit_4]. No mode-commitment diagnostic was recorded, and we claim no mechanism for the dispersion [limit_4].

## Conclusion

We asked whether a probe-gated switch from off-policy to on-policy training lowers val_invalid_rate on this task, and the pre-registered falsifying outcome was met: the adaptive-switch arm did not beat the better pure regime, so this experiment does not distinguish the switching rules [claim_hypothesis_not_supported]. Pure_on_policy averaged 9.04 and the adaptive arm averaged 12.88 (lower is better), although the adaptive arm had the narrower spread across seed–fold pairs [claim_adaptive_not_below_pure_on].

The scope is one synthetic task with one tiny fixed student and one divergence, so the results do not generalise beyond this setting [limit_11]. The author has decided not to run further experiments, so the requested reruns (cross-yoked seeds, replayed-rate corruption, mode-share diagnostics, more seeds) were not done and remain the next steps [limit_13].
