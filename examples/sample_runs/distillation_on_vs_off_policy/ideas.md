## 1. Invalid-rate-triggered off-to-on switch, with yoked-step and corrupted-prefix controls  ✅ selected

Train the student off-policy at first (on_policy_frac=0, teacher or ground-truth prefixes). Then raise on_policy_frac to a target value (0.5 or 1.0) once the student's running free-run invalid rate, measured on a small held-out training probe batch every 100 steps, drops below a threshold tau. Two controls test why the trigger works. The first is a yoked fixed-step control: for each seed, a separate run switches at the exact step where the adaptive run fired. If the yoked run matches the adaptive run, the trigger's only value is locating a good step offline. If the adaptive run is better, the online response to the student's own state matters. The second is a discriminating negative control aimed at the exposure-bias explanation. After the same trigger, the run replaces student rollouts with ground-truth prefixes that are randomly token-corrupted, at a rate matched to the student's current per-token error rate. If this matches true on-policy, the late-phase gain comes from generic prefix noise (a robustness effect) rather than from teacher correction of the student's own errors.

## 2. Invalid-rate-triggered switch from off-policy to on-policy distillation versus fixed-step switches

Turn GKD's static student-data fraction into a feedback-controlled schedule. Training starts fully off-policy (on_policy_frac=0, word-level KD on fixed or teacher prefixes). Every 50 steps the script measures the invalid rate of a small batch of student rollouts generated at the training temperature. When that rollout invalid rate stays below a threshold tau for two consecutive checks, on_policy_frac ramps over 200 steps to a target lambda_on. The mechanism comes from DAgger's mixing coefficient beta in imitation-learning KD, which decides when the learner's own states are good enough to supervise. Here beta is driven by a measured competence signal rather than decayed by step count. The comparison set has three groups: (a) pure off-policy, (b) pure on-policy at several fractions, and (c) fixed-step switches at 500, 1000, 1500 and 2000 steps. This tests whether the switch time matters, and whether tying it to the student's own invalid rate beats a well-chosen fixed step.

## 3. Invalid-rate-triggered switch from SeqKD to on-policy distillation versus fixed-step switches

Training starts fully off-policy (on_policy_frac=0, teacher-generated or ground-truth prefixes). A running estimate of the student's batch invalid rate on its own greedy rollouts is tracked every 50 steps. When it falls below a threshold tau, on_policy_frac is ramped to a target value lambda_on over a short window. This adaptive trigger is compared against a grid of fixed-step switches and against both pure regimes. The proposed mechanism is that early on-policy rollouts are mostly malformed, so teacher feedback on them is spent on states the converged student never visits; that gradient is off-target and noisy. Once rollouts are near-valid, the states the student visits overlap with its eventual test-time states, and only then does exposure-bias correction pay off. If this is right, the best switch point should line up with a student invalid-rate level, not with a step count.

- **Direction:** Off-to-on data-source curriculum

- **Gap:** Gap 6 (off-to-on data-source schedules and when to switch), and as a side effect gap 3 (invalid rate as the primary metric).
- **Hypothesis:** A schedule that switches from off- to on-policy when the student's own rollout invalid rate drops below a threshold gives lower val_invalid_rate than pure off-policy, pure on-policy, and the best fixed-step switch. ## 4. Valid-prefix-truncated mixed rollouts: a token-level self-pacing off-to-on curriculum

## 5. Per-sample validity-gated data-source curriculum: on-policy only for rollouts that pass validity

Instead of a global switch, make the curriculum per sample. At each step the student generates rollouts for a fraction lambda of the batch. Rollouts the validity checker accepts are kept as on-policy prefixes with teacher token-level feedback. Invalid rollouts are either replaced by the off-policy (teacher or fixed) sequence for that input, or truncated at the first invalid position and continued with the teacher prefix. The effective on-policy fraction therefore rises automatically as the student improves, with no threshold to tune. The mechanism transfers instance-level curriculum ideas, which order examples by difficulty, to the choice of which policy generates each example's prefix. The variant is compared against the global adaptive switch, a constant lambda, and an inverted control that keeps only invalid rollouts. The inverted control tests whether on-policy value comes from correcting near-valid errors or from exposure to garbage.

## What the idea committee kept saying

1. Do not make an adaptive or state-triggered teacher/student switch the main contribution. DTDD, DASH-OPD and CPD already cover that ground. Build the idea around a control that isolates mechanism and would change how the field explains on-policy gains. Examples: a matched-error corrupted-prefix arm (the student's own errors versus generic noise), or a length-matched random-truncation arm (validity versus prefix length).  _(support: r1:m1, r1:m10, r1:m4, r1:m6, r1:m7, r1:m9)_

3. Do not filter on-policy data in a way that discards the error-bearing states the method is supposed to correct, such as keeping only valid rollouts. Keep supervision at and before the first error, and check that the gate cannot collapse into pure off-policy for a weak student.  _(support: r1:m10, r1:m3, r1:m6)_
