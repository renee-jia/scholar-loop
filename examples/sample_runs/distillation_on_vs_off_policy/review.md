# Review — Does an Adaptive Off-to-On-Policy Switch Help Distillation? A Pre-Registered Negative Result on a Multimodal Transduction Task

**Recommendation:** reject · **mean score:** 2.2/10

| reviewer | score | recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| harsh-but-fair | 2 | reject |
| harsh-critical | 2 | reject |
| open-minded | 2.5 | reject |

## Consensus weaknesses

- Self-consistency, Abstract and Conclusion. The sentence 'although the adaptive arm had the narrower spread across seed–fold pairs' (repeated in the Introduction and Results) is presented as a mitigating positive. It contradicts the Limitations, which state 'with five values per arm the spread estimates cannot rank variance'. Either drop the spread claim from the abstract and conclusion, or support it, for example with a bootstrap interval on the SD ratio or with more seeds.
- Quality, Results. 'The adaptive switch did not beat the better pure regime' rests on pure_on_policy being the better pure regime. The paper itself says 'the sign of the mean difference [pure_on vs off_policy] is not established at this sample size.' The falsification holds against either reference, since -1.12 is smaller than both SDs. The text should say this explicitly instead of framing the result as 'pure_on_policy had the lower mean (9.04 against 12.88)'. Report the per-pair table (5 values × 6 arms) so readers can verify both comparisons.
- Quality, Results and Limitations. No per-pair values are tabulated, yet the text cites individual values (18.4, 23.2, 15.8, 4.6, 2.6, 4.2). Sentence-level traceability requires a table of all 30 val_invalid_rate values with seed and fold indices.
- Quality, Setup. The teacher's own val_invalid_rate is never reported. Without it, the reader cannot tell whether 9–15% invalid is near the teacher ceiling or far from it. The relevance of the 8.8 hard-label baseline is also unclear, and that baseline was not re-run ('That baseline was not re-measured'). Fix: report teacher invalid rate and per-mode output shares on each fold, and re-run the hard-label baseline under the same seed–fold protocol.
- Quality, Limitations ('binomial evaluation noise is of the same order as the adaptive-versus-reference mean difference'). At p≈0.13 with n=500, the binomial SE is about 1.5 points, which matches the 1.12 gain. 'Within noise' was never quantified, yet the registered falsification depends on it. Report per-run binomial CIs, or a paired bootstrap over prompts. Better still, evaluate all arms on the shared 2000-prompt test set, which the setup describes but never uses.
- Significance and originality, whole paper. The two controls that carried the mechanistic question, 'does the benefit depend on the trigger responding... and on the student's own prefixes', both failed by design. The yoked arm used seed+1000, so it was not paired. The corrupt arm used its own student's error rate and ground-truth rather than teacher prefixes. What remains is a single-setting comparison of known knobs with null-to-noisy outcomes, so a practitioner learns essentially nothing actionable. The authors declined reruns, which leaves the paper as an incomplete experiment rather than an informative negative result.
- Quality, Results (fixed-switch paragraph). The confound is real but trivially entailed. Because the cap is at step 1500 and the trigger always fired earlier, the adaptive arm necessarily had more on-policy steps than fixed_switch_1500. The 'no worse than fixed switch' prediction was therefore uninformative as registered. Report actual switch steps per seed, and compare adaptive against a fixed switch at the median adaptive firing step.
- Discussion. The one potentially insightful observation is left as untested speculation: pure_on_policy is bimodal, with three pairs at 2.6–4.6 and one at 23.2, and the authors suggest mode commitment. A per-mode output histogram at evaluation (copy/rev/rev+1 shares), which costs no training, would test this directly and turn the paper into something with a finding.
- Clarity, Introduction and Setup. The registered prediction and falsification paragraphs are reproduced nearly verbatim twice (Introduction and Setup), and the related-work positioning against GKD, DASH-OPD and DTDD is duplicated in the Introduction. Several sentences are garbled, e.g. 'Against off_policy_ref, the same prediction meets only a small gain.' The drafting notes appended to the submission should be removed.
- Clarity, Setup. 'Implementation details not restated in the setup statements are fixed in the frozen scorer... and are not described further here' and 'Hardware is not recorded' undercut reproducibility. The 1200 s budget is meaningless without hardware. It is also unclear whether the yoked arm used the same fold as its source seed.
- Related Work. Several citations (arXiv:2607.29078, 2610.19302, 2609.09123, 2605.22731) carry identifiers that should be checked. Positioning claims based only on 'their abstracts do not describe yoked or corrupted-prefix controls' are weak grounds for novelty.
- Significance (whole paper): the contribution reduces to 'on one 6-token synthetic task with a 2-layer d=32 student, one adaptive threshold did not beat pure on-policy over 5 noisy pairs'. A practitioner learns nothing actionable. The pure_on_policy SD of 8.48 means the non-result is also not a confident negative. Fix: run more seeds/folds or more thresholds, or argue why this specific null constrains prior methods (GKD, DASH-OPD).
- Quality, Results ('so the adaptive switch did not beat the better pure regime by more than the seed–fold spread'): the falsification is met trivially because adaptive is worse on the mean, but the paper never states which SD it uses as the margin. With n=5 and binomial noise per evaluation of about ±1.5 points at 500 prompts, no difference here is resolved. Fix: report bootstrap or paired-t intervals on every paired difference and per-pair raw values in a table.
- Quality, Setup ('seed and fold are confounded'): this design choice is not motivated and inflates spread. It prevents separating optimisation variance from data variance, which is exactly what the variance discussion would need. Fix: cross seeds with a fixed fold, or at least report per-pair values for all arms.
- Self-consistency, Abstract vs Discussion ('the experiment does not distinguish the switching rules'): this conservative conclusion conflicts with the Results' reading that adaptive 'formally satisfies' no worse than fixed and shows a -1.12 gain vs off-policy. Fix: either drop these secondary readings or state plainly that no pairwise contrast among the switching arms is resolved.
- Quality, Limitations (withdrawn yoked and corrupt claims): two of six arms (a third of compute) are acknowledged as not implementing their contrasts. The second registered falsification clause ('both yoked_replay and corrupt_prefix match it') is therefore unevaluable, yet the paper still presents the protocol as completed. Fix: rerun the controls correctly (yoked on paired seeds, corruption at the adaptive student's replayed rate), or state explicitly that the registered test is only half executed.
- Quality, Results/Limitations (the 8.8 hard-label baseline): this baseline is comparable to or better than every distillation arm except pure on-policy, yet it was not re-run. If hard-label training beats most KD arms, the whole distillation framing is questionable on this task. Fix: re-run it under the same seeds and folds.
- Clarity, Introduction: paragraphs on GKD/DASH-OPD/curriculum are duplicated nearly verbatim in Related Work, and the prediction/falsification text appears three times. The mention that 'their abstracts do not describe yoked or corrupted-prefix controls' is not a meaningful novelty claim. Fix: consolidate the duplicates and drop abstract-level comparisons.
- Clarity, Setup (fold construction): several details needed to reproduce the key confounds are not given. The text does not say which teacher mode distribution the student converges to, how many of the 8^6=262,144 inputs the teacher trains on, or whether the teacher itself has a nonzero invalid rate. The latter matters a great deal for interpreting a 9–15% student invalid rate. Fix: report the teacher's val_invalid_rate.
- Originality: the probe-gated one-way switch is a straightforward schedule on GKD's existing student-fraction knob. No new insight emerges since the mechanism tests failed. The untested mode-commitment hypothesis (Discussion) is the only interesting idea, and no diagnostic was collected for it.
- Significance/originality: the only surviving finding is a non-result. The headline adaptive-vs-pure_on contrast (12.88 vs 9.04) has pure_on SD 8.48 over n=5, with no test or interval. So the paper cannot even claim pure on-policy is better; it only shows the adaptive arm failed to win. A practitioner learns almost nothing actionable. Fix: run more seeds (e.g. 20 or more) and report bootstrap CIs on paired differences.
- Self-consistency (Abstract, last sentence; Discussion, first paragraph): 'did not beat the better pure regime by more than the seed–fold spread' is never computed against the registered criterion. The criterion is to beat the better arm by more than the seed SD, and which SD is meant is unspecified. Fix: state the threshold value used and the arithmetic.
- Abstract: it opens with the result framing but omits that both mechanism controls failed to implement their contrasts. Readers reach the Limitations before learning that two of six arms are uninterpretable. Fix: state in the abstract that the yoked and corrupt controls were invalid.
- Design flaw (Setup, sentence 'trains a fresh student from training seed seed+1000'): the yoked arm was unpaired by construction. This is a pre-registration design error, not an execution slip, and it undermines the central question of whether responsiveness matters. Fix: run cross-yoked seeds on the same seed and fold.
- Limitations, binomial-noise sentence ('of the same order as the adaptive-versus-reference mean difference'): the claim is asserted, not shown. With 500 prompts at about 13%, the SE is about 1.5 points. Fix: report this number explicitly.
- Reproducibility (Setup, 'Implementation details not restated… are fixed in the frozen scorer… and are not described further here'): this sentence and 'Setup details absent from the setup statements are not part of the recorded evidence' are boilerplate that defers to unreleased artifacts. Fix: release the code or remove these sentences.
- Clarity: the Introduction largely duplicates the Related Work paragraphs (GKD, DASH-OPD/DTDD, curriculum) and the Setup's prediction and falsification text nearly verbatim. Leftover drafting notes and the 'evidence bindings' footer remain in the submission. Fix: remove the duplication and the drafting notes.
- Related Work (DASH-OPD/DTDD sentence): 'Their abstracts do not describe yoked or corrupted-prefix controls' positions the paper on controls that it then reports as failed. That novelty claim no longer holds and should be cut.
- Non-obvious angle not pursued: the mode-commitment explanation for pure_on's bimodal outcomes is the most interesting possibility in the data. A cheap post-hoc mode-share count on the existing checkpoints would test it, but none was done.

## Strengths

- The protocol was pre-registered with an explicit numeric prediction (adaptive ≥1.0 point below both pure regimes and below 8.8). The falsification rule ('does not beat the better of off_policy_ref and pure_on_policy by more than the seed SD') is applied as written, not reinterpreted after the fact.
- Means and SDs are reported for all six arms (Results, first paragraph). Paired differences are described at the per-pair level, for example that the adaptive gain comes mostly from the single pair where off_policy_ref was 18.4. This exposes the fragility of the mean instead of hiding it.
- The precondition for testing responsiveness is checked rather than assumed: the trigger fired before the cap on all 5 pairs. The authors also flag that this evidence is self-reported by train.py.
- The Limitations section explicitly withdraws three earlier claims (claim_corrupt_recovers_gain, claim_yoked_matches_or_beats, claim_variance_mechanism_hypothesis) and states concrete reasons for each. The reasons are a mismatched corruption rate, non-paired seeds, and identical on-policy batch counts with different spreads.
- Several design details are stated precisely enough to re-implement the arms: the probe seed (10000+seed), the corruption seed (20000+seed), the definition of per-token error p, and the fold construction (5×500 prompts plus a 2000-prompt test set).
- Pre-registered prediction and falsification criteria are stated verbatim in Setup, and the Results walk through each clause (both pure regimes, the 8.8 hard-label baseline, the fixed switch) instead of cherry-picking.
- The precondition check that the adaptive trigger fired before the step-1500 cap on all five pairs is reported. It rules out the degenerate case where 'adaptive' collapses to a fixed switch, though it is self-reported.
- Per-pair decomposition of the paired differences gives concrete numbers: pure_on_policy is 4.6, 2.6 and 4.2 on three pairs and 23.2 vs 15.8 on one, and the adaptive gain is dominated by the single 18.4 reference pair. This honestly undercuts the mean-based reading.
- Limitations are explicit and specific. Seed–fold confounding, the binomial noise scale, the withdrawn controls with concrete implementation reasons (the corruption rate tracked the wrong student; yoked runs on unpaired seeds), and self-reported diagnostics are all named.
- Abstract and conclusions stay within scope: single task, single student, single divergence, no generalization claim.
- Pre-registered prediction and falsification criteria are stated verbatim (Setup), and the paper reports honestly that the falsifying outcome was met rather than reframing it.
- Per-pair breakdowns go beyond means. For pure_on_policy: 4.6, 2.6 and 4.2 on three pairs, about equal on one, 23.2 vs 15.8 on one. For adaptive vs reference: four of five pairs within about one point, with the gain driven by the 18.4 reference outlier. These breakdowns prevent overreading the mean differences.
- The paper withdraws three earlier claims (corrupt-prefix recovery, yoked match, variance mechanism) and gives concrete reasons for each. For corrupt_prefix, the corruption rate was set from the arm's own student rather than the adaptive student. For yoked_replay, the training seeds differ, so pairing is broken.
- The precondition check is stated: the trigger fired before the cap on all five pairs, so responsiveness was at least exercised. The confound between adaptive timing and on-policy duration in the fixed-switch contrast is acknowledged.
- Reproduction detail is high: architectures, optimiser, learning rates, probe seeding, fold construction, corruption seeding and software versions are all given.

## Questions for the authors

- What is the teacher's val_invalid_rate on each fold? What output-mode distribution does the teacher produce under greedy decoding?
- For the yoked arm, did seed i+1000 train and evaluate on fold i? If so, it is paired on fold, though not on seed. Why can the per-fold differences not be reported?
- At which step did the adaptive trigger fire on each of the 5 pairs? How does the fraction of steps spent on-policy compare across adaptive, fixed and yoked?
- Can you report greedy-decode mode shares for the pure_on_policy pairs? This would test whether the 23.2 pair is a mode-mixing failure and the 2.6–4.6 pairs are single-mode commitment.
- Why was the 2000-prompt held-out test set not used for evaluation? It would reduce binomial noise roughly by a factor of two.
- Given that both pre-registered controls are void, what in this paper would change a practitioner's choice between off-policy, on-policy and switched schedules?
- What is the teacher's own val_invalid_rate and output-mode distribution? Without it, it is unclear whether 9–15% is near the achievable floor.
- At what steps did the adaptive trigger fire on each pair, and how many post-switch on-policy batches did each adaptive run receive versus fixed_switch_1500?
- Can you provide the per-pair val_invalid_rate table for all six arms so readers can compute their own paired intervals?
- Why was seed confounded with fold rather than crossing them, given that variance attribution is central to the discussion?
- Does the pure_on_policy outlier pair (23.2) correspond to collapse onto a single mode, a mode-mixing failure, or something else? Even one decoded-mode histogram would address the stated competing explanation.
- Given that two controls failed and the hard-label baseline was not re-run, what is the intended takeaway for someone designing a distillation schedule?
- What numeric seed SD was used for the falsification threshold: the adaptive arm's (2.23), the better pure arm's (8.48), or a pooled value?
- Do saved checkpoints exist for a post-hoc mode-share analysis of greedy decodes per arm? This would test the mode-commitment hypothesis without retraining.
- At what steps did the adaptive trigger fire on each pair? How do those steps relate to the pure_on_policy outcomes on the same folds?
- Why was the yoked arm assigned seed+1000 rather than the same seed? Was the intention to break the dependence on initialisation?
- Is the 8.8 hard-label baseline from the same student, folds and step budget? If so, every distillation arm underperforms plain hard-label training, which is itself a notable finding worth discussing.

## Reviewer summaries

- **harsh-but-fair**: The paper reports a pre-registered six-arm experiment on a synthetic transduction task: L=6 symbols over an 8-symbol alphabet, with three valid output modes. A 2-layer d_model-32 student is distilled with forward KL from a frozen teacher. The question is whether a probe-gated, one-way switch from off-policy to on-policy prefixes (adaptive_tau25) lowers val_invalid_rate against three comparators: pure off-policy, pure on-policy and a fixed switch at step 1500. Two mechanism controls were planned, a yoked replay and a corrupted-prefix arm. With 5 seed–fold pairs per arm, the adaptive arm (12.88, SD 2.23) did not beat pure_on_policy (9.04, SD 8.48). Its -1.12 paired gain over off_policy_ref (14.0) is driven by one pair. The registered falsification is therefore met. Both controls were mis-implemented and their claims were withdrawn. Pure on-policy is strongly heterogeneous across pairs, and the sign of its advantage is not established. The authors conclude, conservatively, that the experiment does not distinguish switching rules. The write-up is unusually honest about its failures. However, the experiment yields almost no insight: the controls that would have produced the mechanistic content are void, the comparator effects are within binomial noise, and the scope is a single tiny synthetic setting.
- **harsh-critical**: The paper reports a pre-registered six-arm study on a synthetic multimodal transduction task (L=6 symbols, alphabet 8, three valid output modes). The arms are off-policy forward-KL distillation, pure on-policy, a fixed switch at step 1500, a probe-gated adaptive switch (tau=25%), a yoked-replay control and a corrupted-prefix control. Each arm has five confounded seed–fold pairs. The adaptive arm averaged 12.88% val_invalid_rate against 9.04% for pure on-policy and 14.0% for off-policy, so the registered falsification condition is met. Both mechanistic controls are withdrawn as mis-implemented, and no inferential statistics are reported. The paper is candid and internally mostly consistent. Its substantive content is one null result in one tiny setting, and its key secondary comparisons are uninformative by the authors' own account.
- **open-minded**: The paper reports a pre-registered six-arm experiment on a synthetic multimodal transduction task (L=6, alphabet 8, three valid output modes). It asks whether switching from off-policy to on-policy distillation, gated by the student's probe invalid rate, lowers val_invalid_rate. The comparisons are against pure off-policy, pure on-policy and a fixed-step switch. Each arm has five seed–fold pairs. The adaptive arm averaged 12.88 against pure_on_policy at 9.04 and off_policy_ref at 14.0, so the registered falsification condition was met. The yoked-replay and corrupted-prefix controls were mis-implemented, and their claims are withdrawn. The paper frames itself as a negative result that does not distinguish the switching rules.
