# Key points — Training Budget Sets the No-Augmentation Control for an MLP on 8x8 Digits, and 3-Seed Estimates Overstate It

_Question: The question this paper answers is: which no-augmentation MLP control must a sub-pixel augmentation arm beat on sklearn 8x8 digits, and how far do the 3-seed estimates that selected that control shift when re-measured at 5 seeds?_

## Abstract

1. *result* — We find that all three MLP configs promoted as 3-seed winners on sklearn 8x8 digits rose when re-measured at 5 seeds: exp_0015→exp_0016 from 2.8968 to 3.2712, exp_0008→exp_0009 from 3.1286 to 3.5497, and exp_0002→exp_0003 from 3.2444 to 3.5494. This is consistent with selection-induced regression to the mean but does not establish it. We use the result to fix the no-augmentation control that sub-pixel augmentation arms must beat.  
   evidence: `exp_0015`, `exp_0016`, `exp_0008`, `exp_0009`, `exp_0002`, `exp_0003`
2. *challenge* — The motivating hypothesis is this paper's own extrapolation, not a measured effect: an MLP without spatial priors may benefit from sub-pixel augmentation, by analogy with work showing weak-inductive-bias models need more data [arXiv:2010.11929] and with regularization of fully connected layers [doi:10.1145/3065386]. Any augmentation gain no larger than the observed 3-to-5-seed shifts could not be distinguished from selection noise under this protocol.  
   evidence: `exp_0015`, `exp_0016`, `exp_0008`, `exp_0009`, `exp_0002`, `exp_0003`
3. *method* — Before running any augmentation arm, we ablate the training budget (120 to 360 epochs) of a one-hidden-layer MLP (lr=0.05, wd=1e-3) and check width (128 vs 256) and weight decay (5e-4 vs 1e-3). Runs are staged as 1-seed smoke, 3-seed verify and 5-seed full. Each full run gets one held-out test scoring that is never used for selection.  
   evidence: `exp_0004`, `exp_0001`, `exp_0013`, `exp_0011`, `exp_0018`, `exp_0016`
4. *result* — The best-measured no-augmentation control in this ledger is the 360-epoch, hidden=256, wd=1e-3 MLP (exp_0016). Its held-out test error is 3.3333 and its validation error is 3.2712 ± 0.7247 std over 5 seeds. That validation mean is about one and a half standard errors below the 3.8 baseline, which is not significant. It is also mildly optimistic, because exp_0016 was picked on validation from three full runs. The baseline has no test score, and 240 vs 360 epochs (3.5494 vs 3.2712) is unseparated at 5 seeds.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`, `exp_0003`, `baseline`

## Introduction

1. *context* — The task is 10-class classification of sklearn 8x8 digits by an MLP on raw pixels. The metric is validation top-1 error in %, and lower is better.  
   evidence: `baseline`
2. *context* — Our working hypothesis, which this paper does not test, is that an MLP without spatial priors is regularization-limited on small data and so should benefit from sub-pixel augmentation. It extrapolates from ViT's finding that weak inductive bias demands more data [arXiv:2010.11929] and from regularized fully connected layers in AlexNet [doi:10.1145/3065386].  
   evidence: `exp_0001`
3. *challenge* — Testing the hypothesis needs a control that is both well trained and precisely measured. Neither property was known in advance. The 1-seed smokes of the candidate control sat above the 3.8 baseline (4.1667 at 120 epochs, 3.8194 at 240 epochs), and the 240-epoch config fell below it only at 3 seeds (3.2444).  
   evidence: `exp_0004`, `exp_0001`, `exp_0002`, `baseline`
4. *method* — The question is which no-augmentation MLP control an augmentation arm must beat, and how far the 3-seed estimates that selected it shift at 5 seeds. We answer it in three steps: audit stage-to-stage shifts, ablate budget, width and decay, and then report the selected control at 5 seeds with a held-out test score.  
   evidence: `exp_0002`, `exp_0010`, `exp_0017`, `exp_0015`, `exp_0011`, `exp_0018`, `exp_0016`
5. *transition* — The seed audit comes first because it determines how much weight every later 3-seed comparison can carry.  
   evidence: —
6. *result* — Contribution 1 (stage-shift audit): all three 3-seed winners rose at 5 seeds: 2.8968 to 3.2712, 3.1286 to 3.5497, and 3.2444 to 3.5494. One 3-seed std of 0.0063 became 0.9374 at 5 seeds. These rises are consistent with selection-induced regression to the mean, scoped to these three configs.  
   evidence: `exp_0015`, `exp_0016`, `exp_0008`, `exp_0009`, `exp_0002`, `exp_0003`
7. *result* — Contribution 2 (budget, width, decay): at hidden=256 and wd=1e-3, mean error is non-increasing with budget within each stage. At verify, 240 and 300 epochs are equal, and the full stage has only two budgets. No budget step above 240 epochs, and no width or decay difference at 240-300 epochs, is resolved beyond 3-seed spread.  
   evidence: `exp_0002`, `exp_0010`, `exp_0017`, `exp_0015`, `exp_0011`, `exp_0008`, `exp_0018`
8. *result* — Contribution 3 (the control): hidden=256, wd=1e-3, lr=0.05, depth 1 at 360 epochs gives 3.3333 held-out test error and 3.2712 ± 0.7247 val over 5 seeds. That val mean is about one and a half SE below 3.8, which is not significant. The other 5-seed configs sit closer to baseline: 3.5494 ± 0.833 val with 3.7222 test, and 3.5497 ± 0.9374 val with 3.5 test.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`, `exp_0003`, `exp_0009`, `baseline`
9. *limitation* — The sub-pixel augmentation arms were not run. This paper delivers only the measured control and its spread (std 0.7247), against which those arms must be scored at matched seeds.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`

## Related Work

1. *context* — Inductive bias vs data scale. ViT shows that a weak-inductive-bias model needs large-scale data to match CNNs [arXiv:2010.11929]. Swin restores locality architecturally through windowed attention [arXiv:2103.14030]. Neither paper argues that augmentation can substitute for missing priors, and neither studies an MLP at 8x8. The substitution reading is our own hypothesis, and we supply the measured control needed to test it.  
   evidence: —

Both were validated at large-image scale. We check whether decay strength matters for a small MLP at 8x8 (Results: Width and weight decay).

## Results: How far do 1-seed and 3-seed estimates move?

1. *transition* — This section tests which stage gives estimates stable enough to rank configs and certify a control.  
   evidence: —
2. *result* — Smoke to verify, summarized: 1-seed values are unreliable. Discarded smokes at 3.8194 dropped to 3.2444, 3.3601 and 3.244 at verify (exp_0001→exp_0002, exp_0006→exp_0011, exp_0012→exp_0018). Three different configs gave the identical smoke value 3.8194, which suggests a shared smoke seed; this is unconfirmed.  
   evidence: `exp_0001`, `exp_0002`, `exp_0006`, `exp_0011`, `exp_0012`, `exp_0018`
3. *comparison* — Kept smokes, which were selected for being low, also fell at verify: 3.4722 to 3.2444, 3.4722 to 3.1282, and 3.125 to 2.8968. Selection-induced regression predicts a rise, so this runs against that mechanism. A shared smoke seed or a stage-dependent split could explain it. The one near-exception is exp_0007 (3.125) to exp_0008 (3.1286). We report the smoke-to-verify shifts only to show that 1-seed values are unreliable; the selection argument rests on verify to full alone.  
   evidence: `exp_0005`, `exp_0010`, `exp_0014`, `exp_0017`, `exp_0013`, `exp_0015`, `exp_0007`, `exp_0008`
4. *result* — Verify to full for the three promoted configs (test score in parentheses): 360 epochs went from 2.8968 ± 0.1977 to 3.2712 ± 0.7247 (3.3333); hidden=128 at 300 epochs from 3.1286 ± 0.0063 to 3.5497 ± 0.9374 (3.5); 240 epochs from 3.2444 ± 0.5292 to 3.5494 ± 0.833 (3.7222). Every mean rose and every std grew: 0.1977 to 0.7247, 0.0063 to 0.9374, and 0.5292 to 0.833.  
   evidence: `exp_0015`, `exp_0016`, `exp_0008`, `exp_0009`, `exp_0002`, `exp_0003`
5. *comparison* — These configs were promoted for winning at 3 seeds, so the rises are consistent with selection-induced regression to the mean. With only three configs, each rise within about one 5-seed SE, and unknown seed overlap and split policy, we do not claim the mechanism is established. What does follow is that a treatment effect smaller than these verify-to-full shifts cannot be resolved at 5 seeds or fewer under this protocol.  
   evidence: `exp_0015`, `exp_0016`, `exp_0008`, `exp_0009`, `exp_0002`, `exp_0003`

## Results: Training budget

1. *transition* — This section tests whether training longer lowers error at hidden=256 and wd=1e-3. Comparisons are made within stage only.  
   evidence: —
2. *result* — Smokes (1 seed each, so no spread): 120 epochs 4.1667, 240 epochs 3.8194, 300 epochs 3.4722, 320 epochs 3.4722, 360 epochs 3.125. The sequence is non-increasing. The 120-epoch and 240-epoch smokes both sit above 3.8, and the 120-epoch evidence is a single seed.  
   evidence: `exp_0004`, `exp_0001`, `exp_0005`, `exp_0014`, `exp_0013`, `baseline`
3. *result* — Verifies (3 seeds): 240 epochs 3.2444 ± 0.5292, 300 epochs 3.2444 ± 0.1974, 320 epochs 3.1282 ± 0.3424, 360 epochs 2.8968 ± 0.1977. Every step lies within the larger of the two arms' stds. For example, the 320 to 360 step is within exp_0017's 0.3424 but exceeds exp_0015's 0.1977.  
   evidence: `exp_0002`, `exp_0010`, `exp_0017`, `exp_0015`
4. *comparison* — Full (5 seeds): 240 epochs at 3.5494 ± 0.833 and 360 epochs at 3.2712 ± 0.7247 are not separated.  
   evidence: `exp_0003`, `exp_0016`
5. *result* — Mean error is non-increasing with budget within each stage: at verify 240 equals 300, and the full stage has only two points. No step above 240 epochs is resolved beyond seed spread. The hidden=128 arm points the same way (240 epochs 3.3601, 300 epochs 3.1286, 3 seeds), with the same caveat.  
   evidence: `exp_0002`, `exp_0010`, `exp_0003`, `exp_0016`, `exp_0011`, `exp_0008`

## Results: Width and weight decay

1. *comparison* — Width does not separate within 3-seed spread. At 240 epochs, hidden 128 vs 256 gives 3.3601 ± 0.3978 vs 3.2444 ± 0.5292. At 300 epochs it gives 3.1286 ± 0.0063 vs 3.2444 ± 0.1974, a gap within the larger std; exp_0008's std of 0.0063 is suspect and grew to 0.9374 at 5 seeds. Width was not tested at 360 epochs.  
   evidence: `exp_0011`, `exp_0002`, `exp_0008`, `exp_0010`, `exp_0009`
2. *result* — Decay does not separate: 5e-4 vs 1e-3 at 300 epochs gives 3.244 ± 0.5261 vs 3.2444 ± 0.1974. Note that 5e-4 is the weaker decay, although the run description calls it stronger. This null does not support the regularization-strength hypothesis.  
   evidence: `exp_0018`, `exp_0010`, `exp_0012`
3. *transition* — The certified control. With no knob resolved, the control keeps hidden=256 and wd=1e-3 at the longest budget measured.  
   evidence: —
4. *result* — exp_0016 (hidden=256, wd=1e-3, lr=0.05, depth 1, 360 epochs) scores 3.3333 on held-out test, scored once and never used for selection. Its validation error is 3.2712 ± 0.7247 over 5 seeds. That val mean was selected as the best of three full runs and so is mildly optimistic.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`
5. *comparison* — Against 3.8, exp_0016's val mean is about one and a half SE below the baseline, which is not significant. The other full runs sit less than one SE below it: 3.5494 ± 0.833 (test 3.7222, exp_0003) and 3.5497 ± 0.9374 (test 3.5, exp_0009). The baseline has no test score, so test comparisons with it are indicative only, and the three test scores are not used to rank configs.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`, `exp_0003`, `exp_0009`, `baseline`

## Discussion

1. *transition* — No single knob separates beyond seed spread above 240 epochs, so the control was chosen as the longest budget measured rather than a resolved optimum.  
   evidence: `exp_0002`, `exp_0010`, `exp_0015`, `exp_0003`, `exp_0016`
2. *context* — We hypothesize, without confirmation here, that the wd=1e-3 one-layer MLP is still under-optimized rather than overfit at 240 epochs. This is consistent with reports that regularized fully connected layers tolerate long schedules [doi:10.1145/3065386].  
   evidence: `exp_0015`, `exp_0016`
3. *comparison* — The control's per-seed std (0.7247) is comparable to its margin over 3.8. The reference an augmentation arm must beat is therefore exp_0016 itself, run at matched budget and seeds, not the baseline value.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`, `baseline`
4. *future_work* — Forward-looking sizing (an estimate, not a measurement): with σ = 0.7247 from exp_0016 and a half-point target gain at two SE, a one-sample test needs several more seeds than 5. An unpaired two-arm test needs roughly twice that per arm. Paired seeds would reduce the count by a factor set by the within-seed correlation between arms, which no run measured.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`

## Limitations

1. *limitation* — No augmentation arm was run, so no claim is made about augmentation in either direction. The expected size of an augmentation gain is an assumption with no measurement behind it.  
   evidence: —
2. *limitation* — The study uses a single dataset. The smoke values (3.8194, 4.1667, 3.125, 3.4722) look like whole-image errors on one small fixed validation set. However, 3.1286 at 3 seeds and 3.5494 at 5 seeds cannot be averages of such values, and a 3-seed std of 0.0063 cannot arise when each seed moves in whole-image steps. Split sizes and per-seed split policy are therefore unconfirmed. If splits vary per seed, the reported seed variance includes split variance, which no run separates; a fixed-split re-run is future work.  
   evidence: `exp_0001`, `exp_0004`, `exp_0013`, `exp_0014`, `exp_0008`, `exp_0003`
3. *limitation* — Per-seed error counts, wall-clock time, hardware, test aggregation and any setup items missing from the config are not available in the run records, which limits reproducibility.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`
4. *limitation* — At most 5 seeds were used. At that count 240 vs 360 epochs is unseparated, only exp_0016 clears 3.8 by more than one SE, and the 120-epoch evidence is a single smoke seed (4.1667).  
   evidence: `exp_0016`, `exp_0003`, `exp_0009`, `exp_0004`
5. *limitation* — The 3.8 baseline is a validation number. Its config is inferred from the hypothesis text, and it has unreported spread and no test score.  
   evidence: `baseline`, `exp_0001`
6. *limitation* — Seed overlap between stages, and possibly shared smoke seeds, affect how the stage-shift audit reads. The audit covers only the three selected configs, and exp_0016's val mean carries its own selection optimism.  
   evidence: `exp_0008`, `exp_0009`, `exp_0016`
7. *limitation* — lr was fixed at 0.05, depth at 1, and the epoch range stops at 360. No plateau is established, and depth and dense connectivity [arXiv:1409.1556, arXiv:1608.06993] were untested.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`
8. *limitation* — The main finding would be overturned if more seeds placed exp_0016's mean at or above 3.8, or if the 5-seed rise vanished with full seeds disjoint from verify seeds.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`, `baseline`

## Conclusion

1. *result* — The best-measured no-augmentation control in this ledger is the 360-epoch, hidden=256, wd=1e-3 MLP (exp_0016). It scores 3.3333 on held-out test and 3.2712 ± 0.7247 on validation over 5 seeds, about one and a half SE below 3.8, which is not significant.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`, `baseline`
2. *result* — All three 3-seed winners rose at 5 seeds (2.8968, 3.1286 and 3.2444 became 3.2712, 3.5497 and 3.5494), consistent with selection-induced regression to the mean, with seed overlap unknown. At 5 seeds, 240 vs 360 epochs (3.5494 vs 3.2712) is not separated.  
   evidence: `exp_0015`, `exp_0016`, `exp_0008`, `exp_0009`, `exp_0002`, `exp_0003`
3. *result* — Insight: under this protocol, any treatment effect smaller than these verify-to-full shifts cannot be resolved at 5 seeds or fewer. The control must therefore be measured with as much care as the treatment.  
   evidence: `exp_0015`, `exp_0016`, `exp_0002`, `exp_0003`
4. *limitation* — Augmentation is untested, and the split sizes and split policy need confirmation.  
   evidence: —
5. *future_work* — Next step: run the sub-pixel augmentation ablation at 360 epochs against exp_0016, sizing the seed count from its std (0.7247) to well over 5 per arm. Use paired seeds only after measuring the within-seed correlation.  
   evidence: `exp_0016`
